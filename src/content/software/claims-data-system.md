The national PFAS drinking water settlements against 3M, DuPont, Tyco, BASF, Carrier, Amerex, Archroma, and Nation Ford require each water provider to provide PFAS results, production volume, and maximum production capacity for all past, current and, in some cases, future drinking water sources to the Office of the Claims Administrator. Each number provided needs to be supported by a corresponding document. 

Now scale that across 1,850+ water providers comprising more than 5,300 distinct drinking water sources who all need lab reports supporting maximum PFOA, PFOS and other PFAS concentrations ever detected historically, production volume across each of those sources for the past 10 years, and a document supporting maximum production output, and suddenly the problem becomes huge. Layer in the complexity of water systems, the requirement for each data point to be fully accurate and all claims to be submitted within tight deadlines.   

This was not an easy task, but the EH&E Analytics group is built to solve complex challenges like these for our clients. I led a team that built automated systems and a data pipeline to intake data and documentation from water providers. I had calls with every water provider we represented and my team reviewed each claim, every number, and every documentation before submitting the claims for the water provider. As of now, our clients have secured recovery over $1.5 billion across the settlements and that number continues to grow as we prepare to file claims for the latest settlements. 

## What it does

- An online intake tool where water providers submit their information and documents directly.
- Methods to screen lab reports and production records for eligibility under the settlement terms.
- A data pipeline that tracks every water system and source from first contact to filed claim.
- Dashboards that show the status of the whole effort, and the gaps, at a glance.
- Manual review of data and documents 
- Semi-automated claim filing

Together these supported claims for more than 1,800 water systems across the country.
