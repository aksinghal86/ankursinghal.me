For the last three years, our team at Environmental Health & Engineering (EH&E) has filed claims for public water systems in the AFFF litigation. AFFF, or aqueous film-forming foam, is the firefighting foam at the center of one of the largest environmental mass tort settlements in U.S. history. I have managed much of that work, and this post is about what it took.

## The scale

To date, we have filed claims for:

- More than 1,850 water systems
- Serving about 26 million people
- More than 5,300 distinct drinking water sources
- More than $1.65 billion in claim value, over 10% of the total settlement value across the manufacturers involved

The systems are spread across most of the country. There's a large presence in the Northeast (Massachusetts, New York, Rhode Island, and more) and nearly all of Southern California. There's significant coverage across Ohio, Illinois, Minnesota, and Indiana, plus a large share of Florida and parts of Oregon, Washington, and Texas. It extends as far as Guam and the Northern Mariana Islands.

## Why a claim is not paperwork

A settlement claim asks a simple question: what was in this water system's sources, when, and how much water did they produce? Answering it is not simple. The records are incomplete, inconsistent, and often decades old. Each lab reported results its own way, and each water system kept production records its own way. Every settlement has its own eligibility rules.

Each claim means rebuilding a system's contamination history from those records and then testing it against the settlement criteria. Doing that once is careful work. Doing it for more than 1,850 systems on a court deadline is a data problem.

## What we built

We treated it as one. The team built an information management system for the claims:

- **An online intake tool** that collects data and documents directly from water providers, so nothing lives only in an email thread.
- **Evaluation methods** that check lab reports and production data against each settlement's eligibility criteria.
- **A data pipeline** that standardizes information across systems with very different record-keeping practices.
- **Dashboards** that track the status of every system and every source, so the whole effort, and its gaps, is visible at a glance.

The software did not replace the people. Behind it were hundreds of calls with water providers to understand their systems, a great deal of document screening, and careful judgment about what each system's data actually showed. That meant sorting out what was eligible, what was not, and how to make the strongest supportable claim for each provider. The infrastructure is what made it possible to do that judgment work consistently, at this scale.

## What comes next

Four more AFFF manufacturers have reached settlements with water systems: Carrier Global, Archroma, Amerex, and National Foam. We are continuing to work up and file claims for water providers in these settlements.

We also expect personal injury claims to follow, and we are preparing to bring the same approach and infrastructure to that work.
